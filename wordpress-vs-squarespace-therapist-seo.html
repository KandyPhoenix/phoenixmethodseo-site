WordPress vs. Squarespace for Therapist Websites — SEO Breakdown

The platform under your site decides which technical settings you can reach when rankings go cold, and what a professional SEO provider can do for you.

If you're building or rebuilding your therapy practice website, you'll hit this question early: WordPress or Squarespace? The usual comparison covers design, monthly price, and ease of use. This one covers SEO capability, which affects how new patients find you in search, and shows where each platform stops.

The short version: WordPress gives you more SEO flexibility, and Squarespace gives you more simplicity. In a competitive market, that simplicity comes with limits.

Why Your Platform Is an SEO Decision

For therapy searches like "anxiety therapist near me," "online therapy for trauma," and "couples counseling [city]," technical SEO is part of the work. That means Core Web Vitals performance, structured data (schema markup), canonical tags, crawl settings, E-E-A-T signals, and more.

WordPress gives you direct access to all of them. Squarespace manages many of them for you automatically, which is convenient and also means there are settings you can't change.

The choice underneath: You are trading simplicity against technical control. In a low-competition market, either platform works for a basic online presence. When several practices compete for the same therapy keywords, the platform matters more.

WordPress SEO Capabilities: What You Can Control

WordPress gives you, your developer, or your SEO provider direct access to the site's technical SEO settings. In this post, WordPress means the self-hosted software from WordPress.org, which you install on hosting you choose and pay for separately. WordPress.com is a different, hosted service that includes managed hosting, handles updates for you, and allows plugins on its paid plans.

Technical SEO: The Settings You Can Reach

WordPress puts these technical SEO settings in your hands:

  • robots.txt: You decide which pages search engines are asked not to crawl. You can edit it from the WordPress dashboard with an SEO plugin such as Yoast SEO or Rank Math, or as a file on your server.
  • Canonical tags: An SEO plugin such as Yoast SEO adds canonical URLs for you and lets you set a custom one on any post or page.
  • URL structure: You set this under Settings > Permalinks, with a preset such as post name or your own custom structure, so blog posts do not have to sit under a /blog/ prefix.
  • XML sitemaps: WordPress creates a basic sitemap index at /wp-sitemap.xml by default, and SEO plugins such as Yoast SEO and Rank Math provide their own.
  • Redirect management: You manage 301 redirects with a plugin such as Redirection, which also tracks 404 errors and can save redirects to an Apache .htaccess file.
  • Crawl and index control: You can block pages from crawling in robots.txt, or keep a page out of search results with a noindex tag. One catch: for noindex to work, the page must not be blocked in robots.txt.

SEO Plugins: The Tools Behind the Work

WordPress's plugin library is where much of the SEO work happens. SEO plugins such as Rank Math and Yoast SEO add an optimization dashboard inside WordPress:

  • Rank Math: This plugin tracks focus keywords per page, generates schema markup, and checks content for keyword use, internal links, and readability. Its schema types include Article, Person, and FAQ, and it adds breadcrumb schema when its breadcrumbs are switched on.
  • Yoast SEO: Yoast provides on-page SEO analysis, breadcrumb control, and structured data.
  • All in One SEO: Its Pro version adds local SEO with local business schema, WooCommerce SEO, and a redirection manager.

Schema Markup: E-E-A-T Signals for Therapists

For therapists, we use schema markup to state your credentials in a form search engines can read. On a self-hosted WordPress site you can add any schema.org type, through a plugin's schema tools or your own JSON-LD code:

  • MedicalBusiness schema: This describes your practice as a medical business, with its name, address, and opening hours.
  • Person schema: It attaches your therapists' credentials, license numbers, education, and professional affiliations to your content. We use it to support E-E-A-T on YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) content.
  • FAQPage schema: It marks up your common therapy questions and answers in a machine-readable format. Google's Search documentation changelog says the FAQ rich result is no longer shown in Google Search results, so this markup no longer changes how your listing looks there.
  • BreadcrumbList schema: It describes your site structure to search engines.
  • LocalBusiness schema: For multi-location practices, it describes each location's name, address, and hours.

Why this matters for therapists: Therapy content is YMYL content. We recommend named, credentialed authors on every page. Person schema attached to licensed therapists puts those credentials into structured data.

Core Web Vitals: You Pick the Host

WordPress lets you choose where your site is hosted. Managed WordPress hosts like WP Engine, Kinsta, and Flywheel offer server-level caching and CDN integration. For Core Web Vitals, we work toward the thresholds Google's web.dev documentation lists as good:

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) within 2.5 seconds
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint) of 200 milliseconds or less
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) of 0.1 or less

A Core Web Vitals audit and fix belongs in professional WordPress SEO work, because your host, your theme, and your plugins all affect performance together.

Content Management for Multi-Therapist Practices

WordPress works well for group practices. Each therapist can have a bio page with individual author attribution (which supports E-E-A-T) and separate service pages. Each location can have its own landing page, and licensed clinicians can publish to the blog under their own credentials.

Squarespace SEO Capabilities: Where It Starts and Stops

Squarespace covers the SEO fundamentals, and for practices with basic SEO needs that can be enough. It is a closed, hosted platform, so it gives you less technical depth than a WordPress site you control.

What Squarespace Handles Automatically

  • SSL certificate: It is free and automatic for every domain that points correctly to your Squarespace site.
  • Mobile optimization: Every Squarespace site is mobile-optimized by default.
  • XML sitemap: One is generated automatically at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml.
  • Basic meta fields: You can set an SEO title and SEO description on the SEO tab of each page's settings.
  • Basic structured data: Squarespace generates it automatically for blog posts, events, local businesses, organizations, products, and websites.
  • HTML structure: Squarespace templates generate the page markup for you.
  • Image alt text: You can edit it on each image.

Where Squarespace Has Limits

  • Rank Math and Yoast do not run on Squarespace. They are WordPress plugins. Squarespace's built-in tools cover SEO titles and descriptions, a setting that hides a page from search results, and, on version 7.1 sites, an SEO report that flags missing SEO descriptions and image alt text. Squarespace Extensions, its catalogue of third-party tools, includes SEO tools, and Squarespace states that extensions fall outside the scope of its support.
  • Automatic schema that you cannot edit. Squarespace's help center says you can't edit or remove the structured data it generates. MedicalBusiness, Person, or FAQPage schema goes in as your own JSON-LD code through code injection, which is not included on every plan. Squarespace support does not help with custom code, and the code has to be updated by hand when the page changes.
  • Hosting is run by Squarespace. It is included in every plan, and the platform does not support server-side code. Speed work happens in the page itself, by keeping images small and limiting added scripts. The server side of Core Web Vitals is in Squarespace's hands.
  • No direct robots.txt or sitemap editing. A Squarespace support article states that all Squarespace sites use the same robots.txt file and users can't access or edit it. The sitemap can't be edited either. You can still block search engine crawlers or known AI crawlers for the whole site in the Crawlers settings, and hide an individual page from search results in that page's settings.
  • Blog URL structure. Blog post URLs always begin with the blog page's URL slug (for example, /blog/post-title), according to Squarespace's guide to URL slugs. You can change that slug and the format of the post's own part of the URL.

The trade-off: An SEO team can do work on WordPress that isn't available on Squarespace. That is by design. Squarespace is a closed platform built for simplicity, and some technical work needs access that it doesn't provide.

Which Platform Should Therapists Choose?

It depends on where you practice and how competitive your market is.

Squarespace Is a Good Fit If:

  • You practice on your own in a low-competition area or niche
  • You're just starting out and need a professional web presence quickly
  • You have no budget for ongoing technical SEO and want something you can manage yourself
  • Your primary referral source isn't organic search

WordPress Is a Good Fit If:

  • You're in a major metro area where multiple practices compete for the same therapy keywords
  • You run a group practice where each therapist needs an individual credentialed profile
  • You offer online therapy and want to rank in multiple states
  • You're investing in professional SEO to bring in new patients through search
  • You plan to publish regular clinical content (blog posts, guides) that needs to rank
  • You want room to expand, rebrand, or migrate without platform restrictions

If you move later: Moving from Squarespace to WordPress takes work. URLs may change and redirects have to be set up. Squarespace's export file carries only part of a site, such as layout pages and one blog page with its posts, so the rest is rebuilt by hand. If your market is competitive, we recommend weighing that before you build.

What Professional WordPress SEO Services Include for Therapists

When a therapy practice invests in WordPress SEO services, the work falls into the seven areas below.

The Autopsy: Technical Audit and Foundation Fixes

A full technical audit covers crawlability, indexation, duplicate content, site architecture, internal linking, redirect chains, and Core Web Vitals. On WordPress, you have the access to fix what it finds.

Schema Markup Strategy for Healthcare

WordPress SEO services for therapists typically add MedicalBusiness schema for your practice, Person schema for each licensed therapist (including license type, license number, and issuing state), FAQPage schema on service pages, BreadcrumbList schema site-wide, and Article schema on blog content. When the schema comes from a plugin such as Rank Math, its fields can use variables that pull values from the page, so the markup follows the content when it changes.

Rank Math or Yoast Configuration

Installing the plugin is the easy part. A professional SEO provider then sets focus keywords per page, configures breadcrumbs, connects Google Search Console, sets up the sitemap structure and indexation rules, and enables the schema types that fit each page type.

Core Web Vitals Fixes

Speed work on WordPress includes image compression and WebP conversion, lazy loading, database query optimization, server-level caching, CDN setup, and script deferral.

E-E-A-T Signals for Clinical Content

For YMYL healthcare content, we treat E-E-A-T as required work. WordPress SEO services build it in through author bios with visible credentials on every content page, therapist credential schema on service pages, license number references where appropriate, and internal links from each therapist's bio to the clinical content under their name.

Local SEO for Therapy Practices

On-site local SEO on WordPress includes location-specific landing pages, NAP (name, address, phone) consistency in structured data, LocalBusiness and MedicalBusiness schema, geo-targeted service pages for each area served, and content written for the communities you serve.

Content Strategy and Topic Clusters

WordPress supports a full topic cluster strategy. That means pillar pages on broad therapy topics, supporting posts aimed at specific searches, internal links from those posts back to the pillar, and ongoing blog content.

What SEO services can and can't do on Squarespace: An SEO provider can optimize meta fields, improve content, and add schema by hand through Code Injection. They can't install WordPress plugins such as Rank Math or Yoast, edit robots.txt or the sitemap, or run server-side code.

Cost and Maintenance: Keeping the Site Alive

Squarespace takes less maintenance. You pay a plan price, and hosting and an SSL certificate come with it, so there is no separate host to manage. Check Squarespace's pricing page for current plan prices.

Self-hosted WordPress has more parts to pay for and look after. The WordPress software is open source, but hosting is a separate cost, paid themes and plugins can add to it, and someone needs to handle updates and security. Professional SEO services are a further cost on top of that.

Price is one part of the decision. The other is which platform lets you do the SEO work your market calls for, for searches like "anxiety therapist [city]" and "online therapy [state]."

WordPress vs. Squarespace SEO: Frequently Asked Questions

Is WordPress or Squarespace better for therapist SEO?

WordPress gives you more technical control for therapist SEO: custom schema markup, plugin-based optimization (Rank Math, Yoast), flexible URL structures, and room for a professional SEO provider to do more of the work. Squarespace covers the fundamentals and suits a practice that wants a simple site it can manage itself.

Can Squarespace rank in Google for therapy keywords?

Yes. A Squarespace site can be crawled, indexed and shown in Google search results. The difference is in how much you can adjust: Rank Math and Yoast do not run on Squarespace, custom schema is added by hand as code, and hosting is run by the platform.

What do WordPress SEO services do for therapy practices?

Professional WordPress SEO services for therapists typically include technical SEO audits and fixes, schema markup implementation (MedicalBusiness, Person, FAQPage), Rank Math or Yoast configuration, Core Web Vitals optimization, E-E-A-T signals (therapist credentials, author bios), local SEO implementation, and ongoing content strategy.

Do I need a developer for WordPress therapist SEO?

Not necessarily. Many WordPress SEO tasks can be handled by a dedicated SEO provider without custom development. Some technical work (custom schema, Core Web Vitals fixes, server-level caching) calls for someone with both SEO expertise and WordPress technical knowledge.

Where This Leaves Therapists

Squarespace is a capable platform that gets a practice online quickly and cleanly. For therapists in low-competition markets, or anyone who values simplicity over technical control, it's a sound choice.

WordPress, paired with professional SEO work, gives you more technical control. You get full schema control, plugins that handle routine SEO settings, a choice of hosting for Core Web Vitals work, author and credential structure for E-E-A-T, and content tools for topic clusters.
