Why Healthcare Is Different

Healthcare SEO Plays on Hard Mode

Google classifies healthcare content as YMYL: Your Money or Your Life. That covers pages about medical conditions, treatments, mental health, or anything that could materially affect a reader's wellbeing. Google says its systems give even more weight to content with strong E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness) on those topics. So "write more blog posts with keywords" is not our plan for a healthcare site. We write for accuracy first and show who stands behind the content.

The Playbook

How Healthcare SEO Actually Gets Done

📋

Build E-E-A-T signals systematically

Author bios for every provider with credentials, certifications, and medical affiliations visible. About pages that establish institutional trust. Medical review by qualified practitioners when appropriate, with the reviewer visible. This is where we start on a healthcare site.

🔖

Deploy medical schema correctly

MedicalBusiness, MedicalClinic, Physician, MedicalCondition, MedicalTherapy, and MedicalProcedure schema, mapped to the specific pages that describe them. Structured data tells Google what kind of healthcare entity this is and what each page covers.

🗺️

Set up for the local map pack

A healthcare practice serves patients near it, so local SEO is part of the plan. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity. We cover NAP consistency, Google Business Profile setup with healthcare-specific categories, review request templates, and local schema. See our Local SEO service for the full framework.

✍️

Write patient-focused content, not SEO-first copy

Content answers questions real patients are asking in the language they actually use. "What is IFS therapy?" "How long does CBT take to work?" "Does my insurance cover this?" Written with clinical accuracy, optimized without stuffing. The content calendar is built around patient intent, not keyword volume alone.

🛡️

Keep SEO work clean of regulatory territory

We run our SEO work in a way that stays clear of compliance-sensitive areas: no retargeting pixels on appointment pages, no review widgets we haven't confirmed you're okay with, no analytics configurations that haven't already been approved on your side. If we notice something on your existing site that looks compliance-sensitive, we flag it to you. We don't make regulatory decisions.

📈

Track what matters for practices

Not just organic traffic: appointment requests, directions clicks, calls, visits to your insurance pages, and which content leads to consultation requests. GA4 configured for healthcare KPIs, with tracking your team has approved. Reports in plain English.

Frequently Asked

Healthcare SEO Questions

What makes healthcare SEO different from regular SEO?

Healthcare content falls under what Google calls YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) topics. Google says its systems give even more weight to content with strong E-E-A-T on those topics, so accuracy, credibility, and author expertise come first. Healthcare SEO adds work a general plan leaves out: E-E-A-T signals and medical schema. Healthcare also carries regulatory layers that sit next to SEO. We handle SEO and stop there.

Will SEO work create compliance issues for my practice?

Our SEO work is content, keywords, schema, and technical optimization. It does not need protected health information, and we do not ask for any. The sensitive areas sit next to SEO: testimonial handling, tracking pixels on appointment forms, review widgets, analytics configurations. Those are regulatory territory we stay out of entirely. If we notice something on your existing site that looks compliance-sensitive, we flag it to you. We don't make regulatory decisions. Those belong with you and your compliance advisor.

What is E-E-A-T and why does it matter for medical practices?

E-E-A-T stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. Google says its systems give even more weight to content with strong E-E-A-T on topics that could significantly impact health, which covers a medical practice. So on a healthcare site we make sure content is written or medically reviewed by qualified practitioners, author bios with credentials are visible on every piece of substantive content, and the practice's institutional authority (accreditations, associations, peer-reviewed citations) is easy to find.

Can you help with Google Ads / PPC restrictions for treatment centers?

Our focus is organic SEO. Paid media is available as custom-scoped work if you want coordinated paid and organic. Healthcare ad eligibility, certification, and restricted-category regulatory questions stay outside our scope. We don't make regulatory decisions.
