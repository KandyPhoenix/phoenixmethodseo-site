AI Overviews and Your Rankings: How to Optimize for Google's New Search

Google's AI-generated summaries now sit at the top of the results for some searches. Here's what AI Overviews are, what a page needs before it can be cited, and the steps we recommend.

AI Overviews aren't the future of search. They're the present. Google rolled them out across the U.S. in May 2024 and has expanded them since. We recommend planning for them as part of your SEO, not as a separate project.

This guide explains AI Overviews at a technical and strategic level, and gives you a concrete action plan to optimize for them without abandoning your traditional SEO foundation.

What Are Google AI Overviews, and How Do They Work?

Google AI Overviews (previously called Search Generative Experience, or SGE) are AI-generated summaries that appear above the standard blue-link results for certain search queries. They synthesize information from multiple web sources into a single structured answer, with inline citations linking to the source pages.

Google says that to be eligible to be shown as a supporting link in an AI Overview, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet. This is the point to remember: AI Overviews only cite pages that Google has already indexed. If a page isn't indexed, it can't be cited.

Key insight: Indexing comes first. We check indexation, crawl access, and technical SEO health before we do any AI Overview work on a page.

We focus AI Overview work on informational queries: questions that begin with "how," "what," "why," "when," and "which." We treat transactional and navigational searches as a lower priority for this work.

The Zero-Click Problem, and Why Being Cited Matters

Let's be direct: an AI Overview can answer a question on the results page itself. When a user types "what is bounce rate" and gets a complete answer in the AI Overview, that user may not click through to any organic result. That is the zero-click problem.

Here's the other side: a page cited within the AI Overview has a link inside the answer itself. Google says that when people click from search results pages with AI Overviews, those clicks are higher quality.

The strategic shift isn't to abandon traditional SEO. It's to add a second objective: don't just rank. Get cited.

Pages Cited in AI Overview

  • Linked inside the AI Overview itself
  • Your page is named as a source in the answer
  • The reader can reach you without scrolling to the regular results

Uncited Pages Below AI Overview

  • Listed below the AI Overview when one shows at the top
  • The reader may get the answer before reaching your result
  • Not named in the AI summary

What We Check Before a Page Can Be an AI Overview Source

Google has not published a ranking formula for AI Overview citations. The six checks below are our practice, not Google's rules. They are the same things we check for search in general.

1. Strong E-E-A-T Signals

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness aren't just a content quality checkbox. We start here. We check that the page shows first-hand experience with the topic.

What this looks like in practice: named authors with verifiable credentials, content that references original data or direct professional experience, external citations to authoritative sources, and trust signals like SSL, clear contact information, and professional domain presence.

2. Direct, Concise Answers in the First 40–60 Words of Each Section

AI Overviews are summaries. We recommend writing so a clean, accurate answer is easy to lift out: answer the implicit question within the first 40–60 words of each heading section, then expand with supporting detail below.

Don't bury your core answer after three paragraphs of preamble. Lead with the answer. Support it with depth. That structure is easier for a reader to use.

Template to use: [Direct answer in one or two sentences.] [Supporting context: why, how, when.] [Specific example or data point.] This format puts the direct answer first and still gives more to visitors who read deeper.

3. Clear Heading Hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3)

We check the heading structure next. A clean heading hierarchy (one H1, logically ordered H2 sections, H3 sub-topics) shows that your content is well-organized and that each section covers a distinct, answerable topic.

Flat content with no sub-headings, or heading structures that jump from H1 to H4, are harder for a reader to follow. Structure your article the way a well-organized reference document would be structured: scannable, hierarchical, and complete.

4. FAQ Sections and Structured Schema Markup

We add a FAQ section with FAQPage schema markup to pages that answer questions. Correctly implemented FAQPage schema gives search engines a machine-readable list of question-and-answer pairs.

You don't need to turn your entire page into a FAQ. Add a structured FAQ section at the bottom of relevant articles with 4–6 questions that reflect actual search queries in your space. Mark it up with FAQPage schema.

5. Topical Authority, Not Just Individual Pages

We don't look at a page in isolation. We look at how well the whole site covers the topic. A single well-written blog post on a site that has never otherwise covered the topic gives a reader less than a comprehensive resource on a site that has covered that topic from multiple angles.

This is the argument for content clusters: a pillar page on a broad topic, supported by multiple in-depth articles on related sub-topics, all internally linked. Each piece adds to the site's coverage of the topic.

6. Technical Health: Indexation, Speed, and Mobile

You cannot be cited in an AI Overview if Google hasn't indexed your page. We treat crawl accessibility, Core Web Vitals, mobile responsiveness, and clean URL structures as the foundation. They don't cause a citation on their own. They come first.

Priority checks: confirm your page is indexed in Google Search Console, verify there are no noindex tags blocking your content, and check that your page loads in under 2.5 seconds on mobile.

The 7-Step AI Overview Optimization Action Plan

Here is a prioritized, sequential action plan for optimizing your existing content and new content for AI Overview citation eligibility.

Audit Your Indexation

In Google Search Console, check the Indexing report. Identify any important pages returning noindex errors or crawl blocks. AI Overviews only cite indexed pages, so indexation comes first. Fix crawl errors before any content work.

Identify Your Informational Query Targets

Use Google Search Console's Performance report to find queries where your pages currently rank. Look for question-style queries: "how to," "what is," "why does," "best way to." Search them in Google to see whether an AI Overview shows and whether your page is cited. Start with the ones where it isn't.

Restructure Content to Lead with Answers

For each target page, rewrite the first 40–60 words after every H2 and H3 to directly answer what that section header implies. Don't pad. Answer, then support. Use the template: direct answer → supporting context → specific example or data.

Implement FAQ Schema on High-Value Pages

Add a FAQ section to your top informational posts and service pages. Include 4–6 questions that reflect real search queries (use "People Also Ask" in Google and your Search Console query data). Implement FAQPage schema markup correctly. Validate in Google's Rich Results Test before publishing.

Strengthen E-E-A-T Signals Across Your Site

Give every key page a named, credentialed author. Add an About the Author section. Link to author professional profiles where relevant. For YMYL topics (health, finance, legal), include license numbers, certifications, and institutional affiliations. Do this across the whole site, not on one page.

Build or Expand Your Content Clusters

Map your core service or topic areas. For each pillar topic, build a comprehensive pillar page plus at least 3–5 supporting articles covering related sub-questions. Interlink them deliberately. Depth and breadth on the topic are the goal.

Monitor AI Overview Appearances and Iterate

Search your target questions in Google on a regular schedule. Track which of your pages are being cited and on which queries. Work out what those pages do well and repeat it across your site.

What Not to Do: Common AI Overview Optimization Mistakes

A few tactics that sound logical but that we recommend avoiding.

  • Keyword stuffing answers: Forcing target keywords into every sentence creates unnatural text that is hard to read. Write answers for human comprehension first. Keyword density is secondary.
  • Adding FAQ schema without real answers: Thin FAQ answers (one sentence with no depth) do not help the reader. Write answers that are useful on their own.
  • Over-optimizing a single page in isolation: We recommend building out the whole topic on your site, not polishing a single page on an otherwise thin site.
  • Using nosnippet to "protect" content: Google lists nosnippet as one of the controls that limit what Search shows from your pages. If your goal is to be cited, don't use it.
  • Chasing AI Overview rankings at the expense of traditional SEO: AI Overview optimization and traditional SEO are not in conflict. We do the same work for both: authority, relevance, technical health, content quality. Don't sacrifice one for the other.

Frequently Asked Questions About AI Overviews

What are Google AI Overviews?

Google AI Overviews are AI-generated summary responses that appear at the top of search results pages for certain queries. They synthesize information from multiple web sources and present a direct answer before the traditional blue-link results. AI Overviews were previously called Search Generative Experience (SGE) and rolled out broadly in the U.S. in May 2024.

How do I get my content cited in Google AI Overviews?

Google says a page must be indexed, and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, to appear as a supporting link in an AI Overview. Beyond that, we recommend: strong E-E-A-T signals (named authors with verifiable credentials), direct answers in the first 40–60 words of each section, FAQ schema markup, comprehensive topical coverage, and clean technical health (indexed, fast, mobile-friendly). We start with informational queries: "how to," "what is," "why does."

Do AI Overviews hurt organic click-through rates?

They can. When an AI Overview answers the question on the results page, a reader may not click through to any result. Google says that when people do click from a results page with an AI Overview, those clicks are higher quality. Our approach: aim to be a cited source, not just a ranked result.

What content format works best for AI Overview visibility?

The content formats we recommend: direct question-and-answer sections, bulleted and numbered lists, definition-style opening paragraphs, structured how-to content, and FAQ sections with schema markup. Keep your primary answer within the first 40–60 words of each heading section.

Can I opt out of appearing in Google AI Overviews?

Yes. Google lists the nosnippet meta robots tag and the data-nosnippet HTML attribute among the controls that limit what Search, AI Overviews included, shows from your pages. Opting out also means your page can't be cited as a source. Unless you have a reason to block it, we recommend staying eligible.

Does AI Overview optimization conflict with traditional SEO?

No. We do the same core work for both: E-E-A-T, content quality, technical health, authoritative backlinks, and topical authority. Treat AI Overview optimization as an extension of your existing SEO strategy, not a replacement for it.
